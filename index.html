Common questions

Water utility, explained plainly

Hot tapping connects a new branch pipe to an existing water main while it stays live and pressurized. A tapping machine cuts into the main through a sealed fitting, so homes and businesses keep their water — no shutdown, no boil-water notice, no disruption.
Pressurized water escaping a pipe makes a distinct sound. Our technicians use acoustic listening equipment and pressure analysis to trace that sound through the ground and pinpoint the leak's location — usually within a few feet. You dig one precise hole instead of trenching a whole street.
Every new water main should be pressure tested before it goes into service, to prove the pipe and joints hold under operating pressure. Existing mains get tested when there's a suspected failure, after repairs, or as part of a condition assessment program.
New or repaired mains must be disinfected before carrying drinking water. Swabbing physically cleans the pipe interior, then chlorination kills any remaining bacteria. Water samples confirm it's safe — only then does the main go live.
Sediment builds up in every reservoir over time, affecting water quality. Most operators clean on a regular maintenance cycle — if you can't remember the last cleaning, it's overdue. We clean both in-ground and above-ground reservoirs.
Yes — we regularly work with city officials, municipal offices, strata boards, contractors and Band councils, servicing both big and small communities across BC. Call us to discuss your project or maintenance program.
